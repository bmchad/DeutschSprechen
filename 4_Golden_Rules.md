### The Comma Does Not Save You

**Promoted:** 2026-09-21 · **Trigger:** 6 hits, 100% rate, session 1

In English, a fronted phrase is followed by a comma and then the subject. **In German the verb still has to be second.** The fronted phrase *is* position 1, so the subject moves behind the verb.

- ❌ `Zum Beispiel, ein Handy kann KI benutzen.`
- ✅ `Zum Beispiel **kann** ein Handy KI benutzen.`
- ❌ `In den Morgen, KI wird wichtig sein.`
- ✅ `In Zukunft **wird** KI wichtig sein.`

**English bridge:** English *used* to do this — *"Never **have I** seen such a thing"*, *"Only then **did he** realise"*. You already invert after a fronted phrase in those. German does it **every single time**, not just for drama.

**Self-check:** count to the verb. If it isn't word two, something is wrong.

### `gerade` Is Not Duct Tape

**Promoted:** 2026-09-21 · **Trigger:** 7 hits, session 1

`gerade` means **"right now"** or **"just this moment"**. It is not a general-purpose filler, and you used it for five different English words it cannot carry.

| You meant | You wrote | Correct |
|:--|:--|:--|
| currently / today | gerade | heute, zurzeit |
| even (not even thinking) | gerade | überhaupt, auch nur |
| -ing (becoming) | gerade | *(nothing — German has no continuous)* |
| by (by explaining) | gerade | indem |
| thereby / so that | gerade | sodass, wodurch |

- ❌ `KI ist gerade mehr und mehr wichtig gewird.`
- ✅ `KI **wird heute immer wichtiger**.`

**When `gerade` IS right.** Only when "right this second" fits. Pick by window size:

| Window | Word |
|:--|:--|
| This second | **gerade** — *Ich esse gerade.* |
| These weeks/months | **zurzeit**, **momentan** |
| Nowadays | **heute**, **heutzutage** |
| Timeless | *(nothing)* |

It is **not** a continuous marker — German has none. `Ich lese` already covers "I read" and "I am reading". Position: mid-sentence after the verb, never fronted.

**The habit underneath:** when English has a word you can't place, you reach for `gerade`. Better move — leave the gap and mark it: `(even)`. The Parentheses Rule exists for exactly this, and an honest gap teaches you more than a wrong word.
